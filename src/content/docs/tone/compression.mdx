A cappella relies on dynamics. Over-compressing flattens the musicality. The aim is to keep quiet passages audible on a phone speaker without squashing the climaxes.

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="No compression">
    Just set the overall level and limit peaks at the end (see [Loudness](/logic-choir-workflow/export/loudness/)).
    - ✅ The most natural sound. A good default for classical-style repertoire.
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Riding gain (automation)">
    Use volume automation to lift very quiet sections by 1–3 dB, or tame a sudden fortissimo of the whole group.
    - ✅ Musical, because you decide where. The classical engineer's approach.
    - ❌ Takes time, but songs are short.
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Gentle bus compression">
    Logic **Compressor**, *Classic VCA* or *Platinum Digital*: ratio **1.5–2:1**, slow attack (30 ms+), auto or medium release, **1–3 dB** of gain reduction at the loudest points.
    - ✅ Glues the sound and is quick.
    - ❌ Easy to overdo, and it brings up audience noise in quiet bits.
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

<Aside type="note" title="Piercing high notes?">
If the issue is *particular voices* jumping out on loud high notes, rather than the overall level, use a **dynamic EQ** instead. See [Taming loud high notes](/logic-choir-workflow/tone/dynamic-eq/). This replaced riding volume automation for that job on 2026-10-03.
</Aside>
