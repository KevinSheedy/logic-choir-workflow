How a song starts and ends sets the tone: does it sound like a *live record* or a *clean track*?

Our applause is **always very loud**, often louder than anything the choir sings. So the applause gets turned down first, and the house style is chosen after that.

## Why loud applause causes problems

- **Normalising goes wrong.** Logic's *Normalize Region Gain* turns the region up or down so its loudest **peak** hits a set level. If the region includes applause, a clap sets the gain, and the singing ends up too quiet.
- **Effects still hear the loud applause.** A track's volume fader and its automation come **after** the plugins on the channel. The compressor, dynamic EQ, reverb and limiter react to the full-volume claps even if the automation turns them down. **Region gain** comes **before** the plugins, so that's where the applause should be turned down.
- **The loudness reading comes out too high.** The Loudness Meter averages everything that plays, applause included, so a song with loud applause reads louder than it is. Streaming services measure the whole file too.

## Taming the applause

Do this for each song, **after** the concert-wide gain is set (see [Logic project setup](/logic-choir-workflow/ingest/logic-project-setup/)) and **before** judging any processing or loudness.

<Steps>
1. **Split at the first clap.** Put the playhead just before the first clap and split (`Cmd`‑`T`), so the applause has its own region.
2. **Turn the applause region down** with region gain (Region inspector → *Gain*). Start at about **−10 dB**. Adjust by ear until it sounds like the audience in the room, not louder than the choir.
3. **Crossfade the split** if the applause starts before the final chord has finished. Add a short crossfade of about **50–200 ms** so the drop in level isn't heard as a step. The chord's decay carries on underneath, just quieter.
4. **Then apply the house style** (below) to the turned-down applause region.
</Steps>

<Aside type="caution" title="Don't normalise the applause region">
Normalising an applause region usually turns it **up** to full scale, which is the opposite of what's needed. Turn it down with region gain instead.
</Aside>

<Aside type="tip">
If the applause sounds smeared or splashy once it's turned down, lower its reverb send. The singing doesn't need to change.
</Aside>

## Ending style

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="Fade out the applause">
    Let the last chord ring out and the room decay. Turn the applause region down, then **fade it out over 2–4 s**.
    - ✅ Keeps the live energy without overstaying it. Good for YouTube or the website.
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Cut before applause">
    End at the natural decay, just before the first clap. Use **room tone** to extend the tail if the applause starts too soon.
    - ✅ A clean "studio-like" track for streaming.
    - ❌ Hard if applause overlaps the final chord. In that case, use the turned-down applause with a short fade instead.
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Keep full applause">
    The whole applause, turned down, with a fade at the end.
    - ✅ A full live document. Good for the group's own archive.
    - ❌ Long applause at full level would dominate, so always turn it down first.
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Starts
Start on silence or room tone about 0.5–1 s before the first breath or pitch note. Cut the announcements and pitch pipe *unless* they add charm. If there's applause from the previous song or the walk-on, cut it or fade it in under the room tone.
