The mics already captured the room. Reverb in post is for:
- **Smoothing** a dry or small venue (church halls, function rooms).
- **Glueing** the voices into one blended sound.
- **Hiding** edits and noise reduction artefacts.

## Setup in Logic (send/return)
1. Create an **Aux** via a **Send** on the concert track (Bus 1). Put the reverb on the Aux at **100% wet**.
2. Set the send level to taste. Start low and bring it up until you *notice* the reverb, then back off a little.
3. EQ the reverb return: high-pass about 200 Hz and gently roll off above 8–10 kHz, which keeps it from muddying or hissing.

## Starting values
| Parameter | Start at | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Decay (RT60) | 1.8–2.8 s | Hall/church character without blurring harmony |
| Pre-delay | 15–40 ms | Keeps the consonants clear of the wash |
| Early reflections | Low | The real room already supplies them |
| High-cut / damping | 6–8 kHz | Natural, darker tails |

## The common mistake
Too much reverb is the classic amateur choir sound. **Check on headphones and on a phone speaker**. If the words blur, cut the send.
